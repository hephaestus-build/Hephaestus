Activity shows the work you and your workspace already do on GitHub or GitLab: what needs you now,
how much you did over a time range, and the work you did it on. It counts and lists that work; it
never scores the work or ranks anyone.

There are two pages, both in the sidebar under **Dashboards**:

- **Activity** is your own page. It is also the page a workspace opens on.
- **Workspace activity** shows everyone in the workspace, or one team.

Every workspace has both pages; there is nothing to turn on. They show the work in the workspace's
repositories. Everyone with a role in the workspace sees it, regardless of what each of them can see
on GitHub or GitLab. On GitLab, pages say *merge request* where this guide says *pull request*, and
the icons and colours are GitLab's own.

Every pull request and issue opens on GitHub or GitLab, where you act on it. Hephaestus does not
change anything there.

## Needs you

**Needs you** lists the open pull requests that are waiting on you, grouped by what they need:

| Group | What is in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Review requested** | Pull requests that ask for your review and that nobody has settled yet |
| **Returned to you** | Your pull requests where a reviewer requested changes, or whose checks are failing |
| **Approved** | Your pull requests that reviewers approved |

Each pull request shows its reviewers, with a mark on each face for where their review stands:
approved, changes requested, commented, or still requested. When nothing is waiting on you, the
section says **Nothing needs you**.

Below it, **Waiting on others** holds, folded away, the pull requests where the next step is someone
else's:

- **Waiting for review**: your pull requests that reviewers have not got to yet.
- **Drafts**: your draft pull requests.
- **Covered by other reviewers**: pull requests that still ask for your review, but where your review
  would change nothing yet. Either another reviewer has already approved it, or someone requested
  changes, so the author acts first. It moves back to **Review requested** once that approval or
  request for changes no longer stands.
- **Reviewed by you**: pull requests that still list you as a reviewer after you approved them or
  requested changes, as GitLab does until the author asks for your review again. A review with
  comments only leaves the request in **Review requested**. On GitHub, asking you again makes it a
  review request once more, so it moves back by itself.

Open **Waiting on others** to see them; it closes again when you leave the page.

**Assigned issues** lists the open issues assigned to you.

Each list holds only the most recently updated; when there are more, a line under it says how
many it shows.

Requesting changes on a merge request is a GitLab Premium feature; without it, GitLab work never
shows changes requested and no change requests are counted.

## Your work over a time range

Choose a range: the last 7 days, 30 days, 90 days or 12 months. The page shows one tile for each
kind of work:

| Tile | Its number | Also shown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pull requests** | Your pull requests that were merged | Pull requests you opened, and yours closed without merging |
| **Reviews** | Your reviews of other people's pull requests | Approvals, reviews that requested changes, and reviews with comments only |
| **Comments** | Your comments | Comments in the conversation of a pull request or issue, and comments on code |
| **Issues** | Issues you opened | Yours that were closed |

Under its number, a tile says how it compares with the period of the same length before, such as
*4 more than the previous 30 days*. It then shows its number as bars over the range: one bar per
day for 7 or 30 days, per week for 90 days, and per month for 12 months, in your own time zone,
from the first day to today. Every day has a faint track, so a day with nothing reads as zero, and
the busiest bar carries its number. Each tile's bars have their own scale, so compare a tile with
its own past, not with the tile beside it. The first and last week or month can be cut short by
the range; hover a bar to see the days it covers. A tile with nothing in the range does not open.

Open a tile to see its category in detail: its total, its average per day, week or month, its
busiest one and how it compares with the period before, then one row of bars for each kind — for
reviews, approved, changes requested and commented. The rows share one scale, so their heights
compare, and the number at the top line of each row is that scale's top. **Show as table** lists
the same numbers for every day, week or month.

When you choose another range, the page keeps showing the previous range's figures, without
their colours, until the new ones arrive, and says **Updating…** beside the range if that takes
more than a moment.

A few rules decide who a count belongs to:

- **A merge counts for the pull request's author**, whoever pressed the button. The same goes for a
  pull request closed without merging. When the author is unknown, it counts for nobody.
- **A closed issue counts for the issue's author**, whoever closed it.
- **A review of your own pull request is not counted.** A review is something you do for someone
  else's work. Your comments on your own pull request do count as comments.
- **Bots are not counted.**

A count says how much of something happened, not how good the work was. Planning, pairing and
helping in a chat do not show up here.

## Timeline

**Timeline** lists the work you did in the range, one row per pull request or issue, newest first
and grouped by day. A row shows what happened on it: *Merged*, *Approved*, *Commented*, or how many
comments you left. Forty comments on one pull request are one row with *40*, not forty rows.
**Show more** loads older work.

The counts in the timeline and on the tiles follow the same rules, so the rows of a range add up to
its tiles. When a pull request or issue is no longer on GitHub or GitLab, its row stays, without a
link, so the counts stay true.

### Copy as Markdown

**Copy as Markdown** copies the whole timeline for the range, not only the rows on screen: a heading
with the range, then one line per pull request or issue with a link to it and what happened on it.
Paste it into a standup note, a 1:1 document or a record of your work. A rich editor pastes it as a
formatted list; a plain text field pastes the Markdown.

## Workspace activity

Workspace activity shows the same tiles and timeline for everyone. Use **Team** to narrow it to one
team; **Everyone** shows the whole workspace. A team includes its sub-teams, and its activity is
counted in the repositories that team's settings include. Where a team filters reviews by label,
only reviews of pull requests with one of those labels count for it. Workspace totals count only
current members.

- **The tiles** count the workspace's or the team's work in the range.
- **Members** lists the members by name, with each one's pull requests, reviews, comments and issues.
  Search finds a member by name or username. The table shows the first 50 members; **Show all**
  shows the rest. It is always in order of name and cannot be sorted by a count: Activity never
  ranks people.
- **Timeline** lists the work, with who did it, and can be copied as Markdown.

Open a member to see what needs them, their tiles and their timeline, with a link to their GitHub or
GitLab profile. With a team selected, a member's counts are that team's, so they match the member's
row.

A workspace admin can hide a member or a team from workspace activity. A hidden member is left out
of every count and list here. A hidden team is not offered in **Team** and does not count toward the
team above it. Hiding someone does not change their own Activity page.
