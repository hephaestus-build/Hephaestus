The Hephaestus Chrome extension puts a **practice review** into the page of the pull request, merge
request or issue open in your tab, right after its description: the comments Hephaestus posted there
for you, with the way to each, when the work was reviewed, and what the review concluded about your
work. The rest — history, evidence, and for workspace admins everyone's records — is one link away in
the app. It reads the same records as **Review activity** in the app.

It is a place to read context, not another place feedback is sent. Feedback still appears on the
work, on your [Practice profile](./practice-profile) and in Heph, as
[Practice feedback](./ai-code-review#where-feedback-appears) describes.

## Before you start

- **Chrome on a desktop computer.** Chrome on Android and iOS does not run desktop extensions.
- **A Hephaestus account** on an instance that allows the extension. If signing in reports that the
  instance does not accept the extension, ask the people who run your instance to allow it.
- **Membership in a workspace** that monitors the repository you are looking at.

The extension is not yet listed in the Chrome Web Store. The people who run your instance either
install it for you through your organisation's Chrome settings or tell you how to add the package
they provide, which needs Chrome's developer mode.

## Connect it to your Hephaestus

1. Open the extension's settings: click the Hephaestus icon in Chrome's toolbar. Pin it from the
   puzzle-piece menu first if you do not see it.
2. Choose **Continue with Hephaestus** to use the hosted service at `hephaestus.build`. Chrome asks
   you to let the extension reach that one address; allow it. If your organisation runs its own
   Hephaestus, open **Use a self-hosted instance** instead and enter its address, which must use
   HTTPS. You can change the instance later from the same page.
3. Choose the provider you normally sign in with. A sign-in window opens on your Hephaestus, which
   sends you on to GitHub or GitLab exactly as the web app does. It uses your normal Chrome profile,
   so if you are already signed in there you may not be asked again. When it finishes, the window
   closes and the settings page shows who you are signed in as. If you close the window instead,
   nothing changes and you can choose again.
4. If your account still has a step to finish in Hephaestus, such as accepting the current notice,
   the settings page says so and links to Hephaestus. Finish it there, then choose **I have accepted
   it**.

### Allow it on GitHub and GitLab

The extension reads nothing on a site until you allow that site. The settings page lists the sites
your workspaces are connected to — `github.com`, `gitlab.com` or your organisation's own GitLab
address — and you allow each one separately; Chrome asks each time. From then on, whenever you open a
pull request, merge request or issue on that site, the extension looks it up on your Hephaestus
straight away (see [Privacy](#privacy)). You can remove any site again on the same page or in
Chrome's own extension settings; removing it stops the extension there.

If no site is listed, none of your workspaces is connected to GitHub or GitLab yet. Choose **Open
Hephaestus** to see your workspaces or ask to join one.

## The practice review on the page

On a supported page, the practice review sits in the page's own content column:

- on a GitHub pull request, after its description and before the conversation;
- on a GitHub issue, after its description and before the activity;
- on a GitLab merge request, with the merge request's reports, before the activity;
- on a GitLab issue or work item, at the top of its activity;
- on the changes of a pull request or merge request, above the changed files.

It starts as **one line**, the same height whatever it says. The line says, in this order, what is
happening to a review right now, if anything — *Review queued or running* — then how many comments
Hephaestus recorded posting on this work for you, then when the work was last reviewed, and whether
that review could not finish for some practices. For example: *3 comments for you · reviewed 2 hr.
ago*. Click the line to open the report in place; the rest of the page moves down to make room, and
nothing covers it. Click it again to close it. The extension remembers whether you opened it while
you stay on the same work in that tab, including when you switch to the changes and back.

While it loads, the line reads *Loading…*. If it cannot start at all, it says so; reload the page.

The report belongs to the extension, not to the page: the page cannot read what it says. It takes the
page's own look and follows it between light and dark, including GitHub's dimmed and high-contrast
themes.

### What the report shows

Opened, the report is one flat list; nothing in it opens further.

- **Your comments.** The comments Hephaestus posted on this work for you, one row each. The summary
  comment, or the file and lines a comment is on, is the link to that comment on the page, where you
  read, answer and resolve it; the report never repeats its text. Below it, which practices it is
  about and when it was delivered. On the changes, comments on lines come first. The list is what
  Hephaestus recorded when it posted, so a comment may since have been edited or deleted. A comment
  whose address Hephaestus did not record says *no link recorded*. A comment posted while the rest of
  its feedback could not be posted names no practice.
- **Your observations**: what the review concluded about your own work, one row per observation — its
  practice, whether its outcome was positive or negative (with its severity) or could not be settled,
  and a one-sentence summary. An observation made before the practice or the work changed says it is
  no longer current. The first five show, and the rest on request.
- **When and where the rest is.** The exact time the work was last reviewed and the commit it looked
  at, when that is known, and **Open in Hephaestus** for the work's review activity: every occasion,
  its history and what each practice made of it. Workspace admins, including an instance administrator
  working in the workspace, also get **Review details**: every developer's observations, what became
  of each piece of feedback, and each review run, where a run can be cancelled or its results retried.

**No recorded comments does not mean no problems.** *No recorded comments for you* says only that
Hephaestus recorded no comment for you on this work. It is not a clean result: the work may not have
been reviewed, or no practice may watch for what happened to it. Likewise, *No review recorded* says
that Hephaestus has nothing about this work, not that it is fine.

## Previewing work from a list

On a repository's list of pull requests or issues on GitHub, or a project's list of merge requests,
issues or work items on GitLab, each row gets a small Hephaestus button after its title. Nothing is
looked up until you press one. Then one quiet line appears inside that row, under its title: the same
sentence the work's report leads with, and links to your first comments on it. There is nothing more
to open there — the row's title opens the work, where the full report is. From a list, nothing
changes: to request a review, open the work. One preview is open at a time: pressing another row's
button moves it, pressing the same button again closes it, and changing the list's filter or page
closes it too.

## Acting on what you see

Reading happens in the report. The one change you can make from the page — asking for a review — is
always confirmed in a small Hephaestus window of its own, which the page cannot cover or reach:

- **Request review…** appears on the work's own page when you may ask for one — you are the work's
  author or an assignee, or a workspace admin — and no review of the work is queued or running. It
  opens the confirmation window, which names the work and the workspace. Nothing is sent until you
  choose **Request review** there. If Hephaestus does not start a review, the window says why, in
  Hephaestus's words.
- If the confirmation window gets no answer from Hephaestus, it says the request may or may not have
  gone through, and does not try again. Check the report on the page first.
- Cancelling a review run, retrying its results, and approving or rejecting feedback happen in
  Hephaestus, from **Review details**.

## How long you stay signed in

You stay signed in until Chrome closes, or at most seven days (your instance may set a shorter
limit), whichever comes first. After that, sign in again from the settings page.

The extension appears in your list of sessions under **User settings** as *Browser extension in Chrome on …*. Ending
it there, signing out everywhere, or an administrator ending your sessions signs the extension out
too.

To sign out, choose **Sign out** on the settings page. Signing out or changing the Hephaestus
address clears everything the extension was showing, in every tab.

## Privacy

- On a site you allowed, the extension sends the address of each pull request, merge request or issue
  you open to your Hephaestus, as soon as it opens, to look it up; and again while the page stays open
  and something about the review may change. On a repository's list, it sends a row's address only
  when you press that row's Hephaestus button. It sends no other addresses, and nothing anywhere else.
- It does not read or send the content of the page, and it does not read cookies. Its requests to
  your Hephaestus carry its own sign-in, not your browser's cookies.
- What the report shows stays inside the extension's part of the page; the page itself sees only that
  a report is there, and how tall it is.
- Your sign-in is kept in Chrome's memory for the extension only and is gone when Chrome closes.
- Your Hephaestus matches the address against the repositories your workspace already monitors. It
  does not fetch the page itself.

[Your data in Hephaestus](./privacy) and [the extension's data handling](./browser-extension-privacy)
cover what is recorded and who can see it.

## When something is not right

| The report says                      | What it means and what to do                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Could not load here                  | The report did not start. Reload the page. If it keeps happening, open the settings from the toolbar icon and check that you are signed in. |
| Could not load                       | Your Hephaestus did not answer. Check your connection and choose **Try again**.                                                             |
| Your feedback could not load         | The report loaded but your comments did not. Open it and choose **Try again**.                                                              |
| Connect the extension to Hephaestus  | The extension is not connected to Hephaestus yet. Choose **Set up**.                                                                        |
| Sign in to … to see it               | Your sign-in ended. Choose **Sign in**.                                                                                                     |
| One step left in Hephaestus          | Your account still needs a step in the web app, such as accepting the current notice. Open Hephaestus, then return.                         |
| Not available for this work          | None of your workspaces is connected to this site, or the work is not in one you can see. Open the report; it says which it can tell.       |
| Followed in 2 workspaces; choose one | More than one of your workspaces follows this work. Open the report and choose one; the extension never picks for you.                      |
| No recorded comments for you         | Hephaestus recorded no comment for you here. See [what that does not mean](#what-the-report-shows).                                         |
| Not refreshed                        | Hephaestus did not answer the latest refresh. The report still shows its last answer and the time it is from. Choose **Try again**.         |
