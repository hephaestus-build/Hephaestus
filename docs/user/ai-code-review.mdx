Practice feedback is feedback on an observable way of working, such as keeping a change reviewable, explaining why it is needed, or responding constructively to a reviewer.

When practice reviews are enabled, Hephaestus uses AI to review eligible pull requests, merge requests,
and issues against the practices configured for your workspace. It records observations first, then
decides whether any feedback would be useful and where it belongs.

The feedback is advisory. It does not approve a change for merge, grade the developer, or replace human review.

## Where feedback appears

- **On the work:** a public, task-level comment about this change. A line-specific point can be placed
  on the diff; an artifact-level point stays in the summary.
- **On your [Practice profile](./practice-profile):** private feedback about a pattern across multiple
  pieces of your work, with at most one open card per practice.
- **In Heph:** private feedback prepared for a later conversation. Heph decides when it is relevant
  and shows it as a separate part of its reply. It counts as delivered only when it is shown.

Not every observation becomes feedback. Hephaestus can withhold a repeated, unchanged, ungrounded, or
already-addressed point instead of sending more feedback.

If an issue changes after a review, its earlier observations stay in the review history and are
marked as historical. They no longer count as current practice feedback or Heph context. A later
review that cannot finish does not turn the earlier result into a new claim. A comment already posted
on the issue is not silently removed.

A workspace admin can mark an observation as incorrect when it was wrong when made. It stays in your
review history, marked with the admin's reason, but no longer counts toward your standing, your
Practice profile or what Heph knows about your work, and feedback about it that had not reached you is
not sent. Hephaestus tries to add a correction notice to a summary comment it already posted on your
pull request or merge request; an inline comment stays as it was posted. If the admin restores the
observation, it counts again and any notice is removed.

## Answer or dispute feedback

Hephaestus does not read reactions or replies on its comments. Every comment it posts ends with a link,
**Answer or dispute it in Hephaestus**, that opens your own reviews of that pull request, merge request or
issue. Each observation a comment was written from can be marked **Addressed**, **Not applicable** or
**Disputed** there. Anyone else
who follows the link sees only their own reviews, so nothing about you is shown to them.

Dispute an observation you think is wrong, and say why. The workspace admins read your explanation on the
observation, so write it for them. While the dispute stands, a later review of the same work does not raise
the same point about the same place again; withdraw the dispute and it can be raised again. A dispute
does not change the observation or your standing by itself.

The admins can then act on it:

- **Mark the observation incorrect** when it was wrong. It stops counting, as described above.
- **Withdraw the feedback** on your Practice profile when the observations were right but what the feedback
  said was wrong. See [Practice profile](./practice-profile.mdx).

Neither undoes what was already said. A summary comment gets a correction notice where the provider lets
Hephaestus edit it; an inline comment, and anything Heph already said in a conversation, stays as it was.

## Pull requests and merge requests

In this guide, **change request** means a GitHub pull request or a GitLab merge request.

### What happens

1. You open an eligible change request.
2. Hephaestus reads the change, its description, commits, and relevant discussion.
3. It checks the work against the practices enabled for the workspace.
4. It records what each applicable practice observed, then decides whether feedback would help. Useful
   feedback can become a summary or inline comment; a review can also stay private or deliberately stay
   quiet.

Review time depends on the size of the work, available evidence, and instance limits. Rather than
waiting for a provider comment, follow the review from your Practice profile. Once the review records
something about your work, it appears under
[Reviews of your work](./practice-profile#reviews-of-your-work), which the chip beside the title opens.
A review that records nothing about your work is not listed there.

### Read a summary

When the review produces a public summary, it can include:

- a practice the work demonstrated well
- a concern that applies to the whole change, such as a missing explanation or an unfocused set of commits
- links to inline comments on specific changed lines
- an explanation of why the practice matters and what to try next

When Hephaestus finds a clear positive practice, the summary names it only when the review prepared feedback about it for this change; otherwise it is not posted, and still counts on your practice page. This is feedback on the practice, not an approval of the change.

If new commits trigger another review, Hephaestus posts a new summary that can describe what was resolved,
what remains, and what is new. The earlier review stays unchanged, so the conversation keeps an honest
history instead of rewriting feedback people may already have read or answered.

### Read inline comments

For feedback tied to a changed line, an inline comment can explain:

- what Hephaestus noticed
- why it matters
- what you could change

Some comments include a suggested code change. Check it before applying it.

### Ask for a review

There are two ways to ask, and both are limited to the work's author or assignees and to workspace
admins. Each person also has an hourly allowance, so a burst of requests is refused rather than queued.

**From the app.** On [Activity](./activity#review-this-now), each of your open pull requests and each issue
assigned to you has **Review this now**, in a workspace that reviews practices. For your own work that a
review recorded something about, **Review this now** is also beside each row of
[Reviews of your work](./practice-profile#reviews-of-your-work) on your Practice profile, and on the
review you open there. Workspace admins also find it on any pull request or issue under
**Administration → Practices → Practice reviews → Work**. If the request is not accepted, you stay where
you are and Hephaestus tells you why, in a sentence — the work was already reviewed within the workspace's
cooldown period, no AI model is set up within the author's [AI choice](./user-settings#your-ai-choice), the
practices that watch it are turned off, and so on.

**From a GitLab merge request.** Start a comment with:

```text
/hephaestus review
```

The command is case-insensitive. Text before the command prevents it from being detected. It is
ignored once the merge request is closed or merged, and whether it is accepted at all depends on the
workspace settings.

There is no equivalent command on GitHub pull requests. Use **Review this now** instead.

### See what was reviewed, and what was not

[Reviews of your work](./practice-profile#reviews-of-your-work) on your Practice profile lists every
review that recorded something about your work, not only the reviews that left a comment. Open one, and
**Every practice** shows what each practice behind that review made of your work — including the
practices that stayed quiet, and why. This is the place to look when you expected feedback and got none.
A piece of work no review recorded anything about is not listed there; workspace admins can see every
piece of work Hephaestus recorded, reviewed or not.

Feedback on the work waits for a workspace admin's approval by default. The setting is
**Review before sending**. Feedback on your Practice profile and in Heph has separate delivery
checks and does not need this comment approval.

A practice's review state and its feedback delivery are separate:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Reviewed** | The practice was measured. It can still have no delivered feedback. |
| **Running** | A review is reading the work. |
| **Waiting** | The review has not started yet. |
| **Skipped** | Review settings stopped this occurrence before a review began. |
| **Not reached** | The practice was eligible, but the review ended before it evaluated it. |
| **Couldn't assess** | The review could not settle the practice from the evidence it could reach. |
| **Turned off** | The practice is off in this workspace. |
| **Not triggered** | Nothing happened that this practice watches for. |
| **Waiting on a connection** | The practice needs an integration the workspace has not connected. |
| **Expired** | The occasion became too old before a review reached it. |
| **Failed** | A review started but could not finish. |

An observation can be **Met**, **Not met**, **Not applicable**, or **Undetermined**. Met and Not met
say whether the captured evidence establishes conformance or a material shortfall. Not applicable requires evidence that the practice does not
apply. Undetermined means adequate evidence was read but a question remains unresolved.
Missing or failed required evidence stops assessment before an observation is created; it is never
a claim about your work.

A review of past work is quieter still: those never produce comments at all, by design.

### What code is reviewed

Hephaestus evaluates lines added by the change request. Existing code can be used as context but is not reported as a new concern.

Some practices use change-request metadata instead of code, including the description, commit history, and review discussion.

## Issues

Hephaestus can also give practice feedback on issues. Depending on the practices configured for the workspace, a review can run when an issue is created, labeled, or closed.

Issue feedback appears as a single comment. It can cover practices such as:

- stating the observed and expected behaviour
- giving enough information to reproduce a problem
- keeping the issue focused on one concern

Closed issues do not receive a new feedback comment. A review that runs when an issue closes — one
checking that the reason for closing was recorded, for example — still stores what it found for you
and your workspace admins to read, but has nowhere to post it.

Hephaestus reads an issue as it is when the review runs, and by then boxes may have been ticked,
unticked or added, sub-issues finished or attached, and the issue reopened and closed again. So the
practice that asks whether an issue's checklist and sub-issues were finished when it was closed ships as
needing human review, and Hephaestus does not review a workspace's copy of it that is recorded as coming
from the catalog. A copy that has no such record is treated as the workspace's own; ask your workspace
admin to switch it off. Results recorded earlier are unchanged.

## Practices and urgency

The exact practices depend on your workspace. Common areas include:

- packaging work for review
- writing actionable issues
- acting on review feedback
- testing changes
- maintainable code
- security and reliability practices

The app shows urgency as **Critical**, **Major**, **Minor**, or **Informational**. Only comments on
the work use urgency emoji. Urgency orders the feedback; it is not a grade or a merge decision.

## Linked documentation

If your workspace connects an [Outline](https://www.getoutline.com/) team wiki, Hephaestus can read selected documents linked from a change request or issue. This can help it use an architecture decision or design document instead of guessing at the missing context.

Hephaestus only reads collections a workspace admin chose to mirror. It does not write to Outline. Connecting your own Outline account under **User settings → Connected Accounts** lets Hephaestus attribute documents you wrote to you.

## Stop comments and reminders

Open **User settings → Practice feedback** and turn off **Comments and Slack reminders** to stop new practice-feedback comments on issues, pull requests, or merge requests you author and Slack reminders sent to you.

A reminder is a Slack direct message about feedback prepared for a conversation with Heph. It is
sent at most once in 24 hours per workspace when Slack is connected, your account is linked, and
Heph is available within your AI choice. It contains a count, not the feedback text.

This setting controls delivery only. Reviews can still run, their observations remain stored and may be visible to workspace admins, and earlier comments and observations are not deleted.

Whether a draft is reviewed is decided per practice, not by one workspace switch: most practices wait
until the work is handed over, and a few are written to run on drafts on purpose. Marking a draft
ready does not always start a review by itself.
