Practice feedback is about an observable way of working.
Examples are changes that are easy to review, explanations of why a change is needed, and useful replies to reviewers.

When practice reviews are on, Hephaestus uses AI to check eligible work against your workspace’s practices.
Eligible work includes pull requests, merge requests, and issues.
Hephaestus first records observations.
Then it decides whether feedback would help and where it belongs.

The feedback is guidance. It does not approve a change for merge, grade the developer, or replace human review.

## Where feedback appears

- **On the work:** a public comment about this change. A point about a line can appear on the diff. A point about the whole work stays in the summary.
- **On your [Practice profile](./practice-profile):** private feedback about a pattern across your work. Each practice has at most one open card.
- **In Heph:** private feedback prepared for a later conversation. Heph decides when it is relevant
  and shows it as a separate part of its reply. It counts as delivered only when Heph shows it.

Not every observation becomes feedback.
Hephaestus can withhold a point that is repeated, unchanged, ungrounded, or already addressed.

If an issue changes after a review, its earlier observations stay in the review history with a historical mark. They no longer count as current practice feedback or Heph context. A later review that cannot finish does not turn the earlier result into a new claim.
Hephaestus does not silently remove a comment it already posted on the issue.

A workspace admin can mark an observation as incorrect if it was wrong when Hephaestus made it.
The observation stays in your review history with the admin’s reason.
It no longer counts toward your standing, your Practice profile, or what Heph knows about your work.
Hephaestus does not send feedback about it that has not reached you yet.
It tries to add a correction notice to its existing summary comment on your pull request or merge request.
An inline comment stays as it is.

If the admin restores the observation, it counts again and Hephaestus removes any notice.

## Answer or dispute feedback

Hephaestus does not read reactions or replies on its comments. Every comment ends with **Answer or dispute it in Hephaestus**.
The link opens your own reviews of that pull request, merge request, or issue.
There you can mark each observation behind the comment as **Addressed**, **Not applicable**, or **Disputed**.
Other people who follow the link see only their own reviews, and nothing about you.

1. Select **Disputed** on an observation that you think is wrong.
2. Under **Why do you dispute this?**, explain what is wrong.

Workspace admins read your explanation on the observation.

While the dispute stands, a later review of the same work does not repeat the same point about the same place.
If you withdraw the dispute, a review can raise that point again.
A dispute does not change the observation or your standing by itself.

Admins can then act on it:

- **Mark the observation incorrect** when it was wrong. It stops counting, as described above.
- **Withdraw the feedback** on your Practice profile when correct observations led to incorrect feedback. See [Practice profile](./practice-profile.mdx).

Neither action takes back words already sent.
A summary comment gets a correction notice where the provider allows edits.
Inline comments and earlier words from Heph stay as they are.

## Pull requests and merge requests

In this guide, **change request** means a GitHub pull request or a GitLab merge request.

### What happens

1. You open an eligible change request.
2. Hephaestus reads the change, its description, commits, and relevant discussion.
3. It checks the work against the practices enabled for the workspace.
4. It records observations for each applicable practice.
5. It decides whether feedback would help.

Useful feedback can become a summary or inline comment.
A review can also stay private or deliberately send no feedback.

How long a review takes depends on the size of the work, the evidence available, and your instance’s limits.
On your Practice profile, you can follow the review even when no comment is posted on GitHub or GitLab.
Once the review records a result about your work, it appears under [Reviews of your work](./practice-profile#reviews-of-your-work).
The chip beside the title opens that section.
A review that records nothing about your work is not listed there.

### Read a summary

When the review produces a public summary, it can include:

- a practice the work showed well
- a concern that applies to the whole change, such as a missing explanation or an unfocused set of commits
- links to inline comments on specific changed lines
- an explanation of why the practice matters and what to try next

The summary names a clear positive practice only when the review prepared feedback about it for this change.
Otherwise Hephaestus does not post it, but the practice still counts on your practice page.
This is feedback on the practice, not an approval of the change.

If new commits trigger another review, Hephaestus posts a new summary.
It can describe what was resolved, what remains, and what is new.
The earlier summary stays as it is, so the conversation keeps an accurate history of feedback that people may have read or answered.

### Read inline comments

For feedback tied to a changed line, an inline comment can explain:

- what Hephaestus noticed
- why it matters
- what you could change

Some comments include a suggested code change.

1. Check a suggested change before you apply it.

### Ask for a review

There are two ways to ask.
Only the work’s author, assignees, and workspace admins can use them.
Each person has an hourly limit.
Hephaestus refuses requests over the limit instead of adding them to a queue.

**From the app.** In a workspace that reviews practices, [Activity](./activity#request-a-review) shows **Request review** for your open pull requests and assigned issues.
For your own work with recorded review results, it also appears beside each row of [Reviews of your work](./practice-profile#reviews-of-your-work).
It appears on the review you open there, too.
Workspace admins find it on any pull request or issue under **Administration → Practices → Practice reviews → Work**.

1. Select **Request review** for the work.

If Hephaestus refuses the request, you stay on the same page and it explains why in one sentence.
Reasons include a review of the same work during the workspace’s cooldown period and no model within the author’s [AI choice](./user-settings#your-ai-choice).
The practices that watch the work might also be off.

**From a GitLab merge request.**

1. Start a comment with this command:

```text
/hephaestus review
```

The command is case-insensitive.
Text before the command stops Hephaestus from seeing it.
Hephaestus ignores it after the merge request closes or merges.
Workspace settings decide whether Hephaestus accepts it.

GitHub pull requests have no such command.
On a GitHub pull request, select **Request review** instead.

### See what was reviewed, and what was not

[Reviews of your work](./practice-profile#reviews-of-your-work) lists each review with recorded results about your work, not only reviews with comments.
**Every practice** shows each practice’s result, with reasons, including practices that sent no feedback.
This explains why expected feedback did not arrive.

1. Open a review on your Practice profile.
2. Select **Every practice**.

Work without recorded review results does not appear there.
Workspace admins can see each piece of work Hephaestus recorded, reviewed or not.

By default, feedback on the work waits for a workspace admin’s approval.
The setting is **Review before sending**.
Feedback on your Practice profile and in Heph has separate delivery checks and does not need this approval.

A practice’s review state and its feedback delivery are separate:

| State or condition | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Reviewed** | The review measured the practice. It can still have no delivered feedback. |
| **Running** | A review is reading the work. |
| **Waiting** | The review has not started yet. |
| **Skipped** | Review settings stopped this occurrence before a review began. |
| **Not reached** | The practice was eligible, but the review ended before it evaluated it. |
| **We could not assess** | The review could not settle the practice from the evidence it could reach. |
| **Turned off** | The practice is off in this workspace. |
| **Not triggered** | Nothing happened that this practice watches for. |
| **Waiting on a connection** | The practice needs an integration the workspace has not connected. |
| **Expired** | The occasion became too old before a review reached it. |
| **Failed** | A review started but could not finish. |

An observation can be **Met**, **Not met**, **Not applicable**, or **Undetermined**.
**Met** and **Not met** say whether the captured evidence shows that the work meets the practice or falls materially short of it.
**Not applicable** needs evidence that the practice does not apply.
**Undetermined** means the review read enough evidence but could not settle a question.
Missing or failed required evidence stops the assessment before the review creates an observation.
This failure is never a claim about your work.

A review of past work is quieter still.
By design, it never produces comments.

### What code is reviewed

Hephaestus evaluates the lines that a change request adds.
It can use existing code as context but does not report it as a new concern.

Some practices look at the details of the change request instead of its code: the description, the commit history, and the review discussion.

## Issues

Hephaestus can also give practice feedback on issues.
Depending on the workspace practices, a new issue, an update, or a closure can start a review.
Changes to a closed issue’s description or discussion can start another review of its current record.

Issue feedback appears as a single comment. It can cover practices such as:

- the observed and expected behavior
- enough information to reproduce a problem
- one concern per issue

Closed issues do not receive a new feedback comment.
A review that runs when an issue closes still stores its results for you and workspace admins, but cannot post a comment.
For example, it might check that the issue records why it closed.

Hephaestus reads an issue’s current state when the review runs.
By then, people may have selected, cleared, or added checklist boxes.
They may have completed or attached sub-issues, or reopened and closed the issue again.
So the catalog practice that checks checklists and sub-issues at closure needs a human reviewer.
Hephaestus does not review a workspace copy recorded as adopted from the catalog.

A copy without that record counts as the workspace’s own practice.
Earlier results stay unchanged.

1. For a copy without that record, ask your workspace admin to switch it off.

## Practices and urgency

The practices depend on your workspace. Common ones cover:

- work prepared for review
- issues with clear actions
- responses to review feedback
- tests for changes
- maintainable code
- security and reliability practices

The app shows urgency as **Critical**, **Major**, **Minor**, or **Informational**.
Only comments on the work use urgency emoji.
Urgency orders the feedback.
It is not a grade or a merge decision.

## Linked documentation

If your workspace connects an [Outline](https://www.getoutline.com/) team wiki, Hephaestus can read selected documents linked from a change request or issue.
It can then use an architecture decision or design document instead of guessing at missing context.

Hephaestus reads only the collections that a workspace admin chose to mirror. It does not write to Outline. Your Outline account link under **User settings → Connected accounts** lets Hephaestus identify you as the author of your documents.

## Stop comments and reminders

1. Open **User settings → Practice feedback**.
2. Turn off **Comments and Slack reminders**.

This stops new practice-feedback comments on issues, pull requests, or merge requests you author.
It also stops Slack reminders sent to you.

A reminder is a Slack direct message about feedback prepared for a conversation with Heph. It arrives at most once in 24 hours per workspace.
The workspace needs a Slack connection, you must have connected your Slack account, and Heph must be available within your AI choice.
The message contains a count, not the feedback text.

This setting controls delivery only. Reviews can still run.
Their observations stay stored and may be visible to workspace admins.
This setting does not delete earlier comments or observations.

Each practice decides whether it reviews drafts, and no single workspace switch controls it.
Most practices wait until the work is marked ready.
A few run on drafts on purpose.
Marking a draft ready does not always start a review by itself.
