Your Practice profile shows where your recent work stands on the practices your workspace cares about,
what held and what changed since the review before the latest one, and the feedback written from it.
Other developers cannot see it; [Your data in Hephaestus](./privacy.mdx#ai-processing-and-visibility)
lists who else can. It rests on practice feedback about your own pull requests, merge requests,
issues, conversations and documents, never on comparisons with other developers.

## Opening it

Sign in and select a workspace: a workspace that reviews practices opens on your Practice profile. It
is also the first entry in the sidebar under Dashboards. A workspace that does not review practices
has no Practice profile and opens on [Activity](./activity.mdx) instead.

## Reading the top of the page

The header says what Hephaestus reviews and how a piece of feedback resolves, and **Latest run** names
the latest review and the work it ran on. Choosing it opens **Reviews of your work**. A ring counts your practices by standing — going well, mixed feedback, needs
attention, nothing to report yet, or not observed yet — and **See all practice groups** opens the
table of every practice group.

Under the header, Heph sums up what happened since the review before the latest one:

- **What is holding up well** — the practices you kept showing, each with how many pieces of work it
  held across, and any feedback your work resolved.
- **What needs your attention** — at most two practices you can do something about today: feedback
  your work had started to answer and a newer review raised again, and feedback that is new. Each
  row says what happened and on which piece of work, and **Read the feedback** takes you straight to
  that card below.
- **What changed** — a practice or group that moved to another standing, one seen for the first
  time, a trend that turned. The paragraph names the most important moves and counts the rest;
  **Show the changes** unfolds them.
- The work the latest review looked at, listed by kind.

The summary describes recent evidence, not your ability. There are no scores, no percentages and no
ranking.

## Reviews of your work

**Latest run** in the header opens every review that ran on your work, newest first and grouped by
day, each heading naming its day: when the review ran, which piece of work it looked at, and what it
found. A row leads with the practices the review marked as problems, by name, or says "Nothing to
improve" where none slipped, and for a review that stopped early, that it stopped and what it had
recorded by then. Under that it counts what the review reached: what held up, what did not apply,
and how many practices it reached in all, which is what those add up to. Where the review left
feedback on the work, **Read the feedback on the pull request** opens it where it was left. The
second review of a piece of work says so under the work's own title.
**Show** narrows the list to one kind of work and **Timeframe** to the last 7, 30 or 90 days; the
list starts on all time and loads the earlier reviews as you read to the end of it. The work's own
title links to the original, and where you may ask for another review of that work — your own pull
requests and issues — **Review this now** is beside the row. Only you can see this list.

**Open run** opens one review: the work it ran on with a link to the original, when it ran, a tag
saying which review of that work it is where it is not the first, and, when you may ask,
**Review this now**. Two tabs follow. **Every
practice** lists each practice this review ran against your work: what it saw about your work, one
line per observation, or else why the practice stayed quiet. The list follows the newest review of
a piece of work; an older review of the same work points you to **What we noticed** instead. The list narrows by practice group, by the event that starts a review, or by name. **What we noticed** lists everything recorded
about this work in order: what happened, how it was noticed, and whether a review started.

## Your feedback

Each card is one pattern that recurred across your work: what was seen, on which pieces of work and
when, and one next step to try. The tabs above the cards split them, newest first: **Newest** shows
the two newest open cards, **Open** every open card, **Resolved and closed** every card that has
finished, whether resolved by the work, marked by you, or closed because its practice's review rules
changed, and **All** every card, withdrawn ones included.

A card resolves in one of two ways:

- **By your work.** Once three pieces of your work in a row come back clean on the practice, the card
  reads *Resolved by the work* and names the pieces of work that did it. A piece of work that shows the
  problem again starts the count over; the line under the next step keeps the count.
- **By you.** Under **Your response**, choose **Addressed** or **Not applicable** on the card; it then
  reads *Marked as addressed* or *Marked as not applicable* with the day you did. Press it again to
  take it back, and the card opens again.

If your workspace changes a practice after a card about it was written, the card closes unresolved and
says so: it reads *Closed*, the line under the next step gives the day and the reason, and the count of
clean work is gone, because nothing can tick a card that is closed. A closed card stays on the page for
30 days after it closed. A newer card about the same practice
replaces the open one, so you never have two open cards about one practice.

A workspace admin can withdraw a card whose words were wrong. If you had already seen it, a recent
withdrawal shows under **All** as a short notice that it was withdrawn, without what it said; one you had not
seen yet simply never appears. A withdrawn card takes no new answer. It resolved nothing and says nothing about how
your work stands, and what the reviews observed is unchanged. What it said is left out of the feedback given
to later reviews and Heph. If the admin restores it, it comes back as it was.

Rate a card **Helpful** or **Not helpful**. Helpful opens an optional note; Not helpful asks for a reason
and a sentence on what was off, so the feedback can improve. Choosing **Not accurate** as the reason
disputes the card: the workspace admins read your sentence on the observations behind it, and they can
mark an observation incorrect or withdraw the card. The card's own text stays private to you. Your other
notes are not shown to anyone.

## The practice group table

**See all practice groups** opens the **All practice groups** panel, which lists every practice group in your
workspace as a row: its standing, a ring that splits the group's practices by standing, and a sentence
on what changed in it since the review before.
The rows are ordered by standing, the groups that need attention first; press the **Standing** heading
to reverse the order.

## Opening a group and a practice

Select a row to open the group: what the group is about, what held and what changed in it, your next
step from its newest open feedback, and its practices with their standing and trend. Select a
practice to go one level deeper: the observations behind it, review by review, which you can respond
to; its feedback; and why it matters and what doing it well looks like.

Each panel opens over the one before it, and the page stays visible behind. The short path above a
panel's title says where you are, from the page down to this panel; select a step in it to go back
there, or use Back to return one level.

## When nothing has been reviewed yet

Before the first review of your work, every practice counts as not observed yet, the summary is empty
and there is no feedback to show. The first review fills the page in.
