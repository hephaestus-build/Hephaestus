Your Practice profile shows where your recent work stands on the practices your workspace cares about,
what held and what changed since the latest review, and the feedback written from it. Only you can see
it. It rests on practice feedback about your own pull requests, merge requests, issues and
conversations, never on comparisons with other developers.

## Opening it

Sign in, select a workspace and choose **Practice profile** in the sidebar under Dashboards.

## Reading the top of the page

The header names the **latest review** and the work it ran on, and beside it a ring counts your
practices by standing: going well, mixed feedback, needs attention, nothing to report yet, or not
observed yet. Hover a count for what the standing means. **See all practices** opens the table of every
practice group.

Under the header, Heph sums up what happened since the review before the latest one:

- **What is holding up well** — the practices you kept showing, each with how many pieces of work it
  held across, and any feedback your work resolved.
- **What changed** — new feedback, a practice or group that moved to another standing, one seen for the
  first time, a trend that turned. The paragraph names the most important moves and counts the rest;
  **See the other …** unfolds them.
- The work the latest review looked at, listed by kind, with the span the summary covers.

The summary describes recent evidence, not your ability. There are no scores, no percentages and no
ranking.

## Your feedback

Each card is one pattern that recurred across your work: what was seen, on which pieces of work and
when, and one next step to try. The tabs above the cards split them: **Newest** holds the two newest
open cards, **Open**, **Resolved** and **All** the rest, newest first.

A card resolves in one of two ways, and it says which. A resolved card stays on the page for 30 days and
then leaves it. If your workspace changes a practice after a card about it was written, the card closes
and says so. A newer card about the same practice replaces the open one, so you never have two open cards
about one habit.

The two ways a card resolves:

- **By your work.** Once three pieces of your work in a row come back clean on the practice, the card
  reads *Resolved by the work* and names the pieces of work that did it. A piece of work that shows
  the problem again starts the count over; the line under the next step keeps the count.
- **By you.** Open the practice behind the card, find the observation the feedback rests on and choose
  **Addressed** under *Your response*. The card then reads *Marked as addressed* with the day
  you did. **Not applicable** resolves it the same way. **Disputed** is the other answer there and leaves
  the card open; a dispute asks for a sentence on what was missed.

Rate a card **Helpful** or **Not helpful**. Helpful opens an optional note; Not helpful asks for a reason
and a sentence on what was off, so the feedback can improve.

## The practice group table

**See all practices** opens every practice group in your workspace as a row: its standing, a ring that
splits the group's practices by standing, and a sentence on what changed in it since the review before.
The rows are ordered by standing, the groups that need attention first; press the **Status** heading
to reverse the order.

## Opening a group and a practice

Select a row to open the group: what the group is about, what held and what changed in it, your next
step from its newest open feedback, and its practices with their standing and trend. Select a
practice to go one level deeper: the observations behind it, review by review, which you can respond
to; its feedback; and why it matters and what doing it well looks like.

Each panel opens over the one before it, and the page stays visible behind. The short path above a
panel's title says where you are, from the page down to this panel; select a step in it to go back
there, or use Back to return one level.

## When nothing has been reviewed yet

Before the first review of your work, every practice counts as not observed yet, the summary is empty
and there is no feedback to show. The first review fills the page in.
