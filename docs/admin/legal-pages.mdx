Hephaestus ships with two legal pages: `/imprint` and `/privacy`.
The webapp renders them from Markdown files that nginx serves directly.
This page explains the webapp's three-layer resolution and each self-hosted operator's responsibilities.

:::caution CAUTION: Configure legal notices before admitting users
The built-in “not configured” pages are placeholders, not legal notices. Use `LEGAL_PROFILE=tumaet`
only for the TUM/AET deployment it describes. Other operators must supply their own notices.
:::

## First-login consent notice

The first-login screen carries no operator-specific text.
It states the terms of use and links to `/privacy` and `/imprint`.
Those pages identify the deployment operator, the data it stores, and its retention periods.
Thus, the same text is accurate on every instance.
These overrides make it complete.

The sign-in and first-login screens have their own `/privacy` and `/imprint` links.
They render without the site footer.
On a deployment that still serves the placeholder, the first-login screen links to no legal notice.

1. Configure both pages before the first user signs in.

When the account holder selects **Save and continue**, the ledger records two facts against the notice version shown:

- The account accepted the terms with **I accept the terms of use**.
- The screen presented this text, which links to the privacy notice.

Both facts come from one checkbox.
The ledger records the text presented to the account and its agreement.
It does not establish that anybody opened the link.

Where the screen asks the research question below, it records the research answer as a third, separate decision.
Either answer completes setup.
Hephaestus records nothing until the account holder selects **Save and continue**.
[Your data in Hephaestus](/user/privacy#signing-in-and-choosing-research-participation) describes the same screen for the person who answers it.

## The optional research question

`HEPHAESTUS_RESEARCH_ORGANIZATION` names the organization that runs research on this deployment.
Its presence determines whether the research question appears.
[Production Setup](./production-setup) lists the setting with the other deployment settings.
The organization does not need to be academic.
Choose a name that reads correctly in the sentence "If you say yes, the organization may use your data for research."

Consent must be informed.
This includes the controller's identity ([EDPB 05/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202005_consent_en.pdf) § 3.3).
Thus, the configured name appears beside the choice on the first-login screen and in account settings, not only in a link.

```env
HEPHAESTUS_RESEARCH_ORGANIZATION="Technical University of Munich (AET)"
```

By default, the variable is unset.
In this state, no research question appears.
First-login setup contains only the terms.
The account-settings switch is hidden.
`PUT /user/consent/research` returns 404.

### What the question asks

The screen asks **Allow research use of your data?** with two answers: **Yes, allow research use** and **No, keep my data out of research**.
The legal basis is consent under Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR.
It is a broad consent to an area of research, not to one research project.

[Recital 33 GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-33/) allows this when the purpose of the research cannot be fully specified at collection.
[EDPB 05/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202005_consent_en.pdf) paragraphs 153 to 163 set the conditions.
The obligations below follow from those conditions.

The area of research is how developers work and learn, and how AI systems can review and support that work.
It includes building and running benchmarks and evaluation datasets for such AI systems.
The data it may use is:

- The participant's work in the tools that the project connects.
- Hephaestus observations and feedback about that work.
- The participant's responses to that feedback.
- How the participant uses Hephaestus, including conversations with Heph and answers to research surveys.

Sign-in credentials and access tokens are never used.
Slack messages that a participant stops Hephaestus from using stay out of research too.

The consent is optional and separate from the terms.
Saying no or withdrawing has no disadvantage ([Art. 7(4) GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-7-gdpr/)).

Research that falls outside this area is not covered by this consent.
For such research, change the wording with a new `WORDING_VERSION` after legal review.

### A change is a new question

**A change to the organization name is a new question. Hephaestus treats it as one.**
Every research decision records the organization that the question named in `consent_decision.research_organization`.
Consent to research is consent to the organization that runs it.
After a rename, no other record identifies the organization that the account saw in the question.

If the setting has a different name, no account's grant to the previous one authorizes anything.
`participatesInResearch` reports false.
Setup asks the research question again.
Earlier decisions stay in the ledger as a record of the questions and answers at that time.
Terms acceptance stays unchanged because the terms did not change.

**A change to the wording is a new question for every account.**
A new `WORDING_VERSION` is a new notice version.
Every account sees the setup screen once more, with the terms and, where an organization is named, the research question.
An earlier yes is not carried over to a broader scope.
Only a decision on the current version and the current organization authorizes anything.

Until an account answers, `participatesInResearch` reports false and research survey invitations stop.

Even a cosmetic rename gives every account one more setup screen.
Make a change to the name or the wording deliberately.

Both the setup screen and the settings switch return the organization that they displayed.
If a submission names an organization that this instance no longer runs, Hephaestus refuses it with `409`.
A tab left open across a change cannot record a decision about an organization that its reader never saw.

Research-purpose surveys and their email invitations require a current grant for the configured
organization. Withdrawal stops new research survey participation and invitation delivery. It does not
stop ordinary practice reviews or erase rows in the instance database. The research team removes the data from its datasets that are not yet anonymized. The Slack App Home
links to account settings rather than maintaining a separate research decision.

### Withdrawal

A participant withdraws with one switch, **Allow research use of my data**, in **User settings → Research participation**.
This is as easy as giving consent ([Art. 7(3) GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-7-gdpr/)).
New research use stops, and research survey invitations stop.
The research organization removes the participant's data from datasets that are not yet anonymized.
Research done before the withdrawal stays lawful.

Anonymized data in a published result or dataset cannot be traced back and cannot be removed.

Withdrawal does not erase other stored data by itself.
Account erasure is a separate request.

### No separate consent for datasets

The consent has no second choice for sharing or publishing datasets.
Only anonymized data leaves the research team.
Anonymous data is outside the GDPR ([Recital 26](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-26/)).
Paragraph 47 of [EDPB Guidelines 1/2026](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-05/edpb_guidelines_202601_scientificresearch_en.pdf) (draft, adopted 15 April 2026) lets a broad consent cover the provision of data within the research area.

A release needs a separate choice and a new wording version when it would do any of the following:

- Contain data that is not anonymized.
- Serve a purpose outside the stated area of research.
- Go to a third country without adequate safeguards.

### What operators must do

The broad consent is valid only if the operator meets these obligations.
Meet all of them before you set `HEPHAESTUS_RESEARCH_ORGANIZATION`, and keep to them while the research runs.

1. Before any research export, analysis, or benchmark run, apply the latest `RESEARCH_PARTICIPATION` decision of each account.
   Use only a grant for the current notice version and the current organization.
2. Remove the data of withdrawn accounts from datasets that are not yet anonymized without undue delay.
   Treat an erasure request for data in such a dataset the same way.
   Treat a Slack message-use opt-out the same way for the affected messages.
3. Keep participants regularly informed about the research.
   Give the current research purpose, working methods, projects and released datasets.
   Give a way to receive updates as the research progresses.
   Use a research information page or a named research contact who answers questions and sends these updates.
   An email address that only waits for questions does not meet this duty.
   See [EDPB 05/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202005_consent_en.pdf) paragraphs 161 and 162 and footnote 74.
   See also [EDPB 1/2026](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-05/edpb_guidelines_202601_scientificresearch_en.pdf) (draft) paragraphs 48 and 49.
4. Assess whether the research needs an ethics committee vote.
   Get the approval where your institution or the research process requires it.
   Do not state an approval in the notice that you do not hold.
   A vote is not required for every study ([TUM ethics committee](https://www.tum.de/en/about-tum/organization/ethics-committee)).
5. Keep the research team separate from grading and line management.
   A power imbalance can weaken the free choice of a participant.
   See [EDPB 05/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202005_consent_en.pdf) paragraphs 16 to 24 and 46 to 48.
   See also [Recital 43](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-43/).
   Instance admins can read research survey answers, so give the admin role only to people who meet this rule.
6. Test a dataset for re-identification before you call it anonymous.
   Release it only if the test passes.
   Free text and code are hard to anonymize ([EDPB 1/2026](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-05/edpb_guidelines_202601_scientificresearch_en.pdf) paragraph 164).
7. Honor the AI choice of each participant in benchmark runs.
   Model providers may see only pseudonymized data, under a data processing agreement.
8. Do not use the consent for browsing activity from the browser extension.
   The privacy notice already says so.
9. Keep each safeguard that the consent text states.
   The research team replaces identifiers with a code, stores the key apart, and screens text for names.
   It restricts access to the team and its processors.
   It sends model providers only pseudonymized data, under a data processing agreement.

Publish the retention and recipient details of the research in your privacy notice.
The bundled TUM notice shows one way to write these details.

`ConsentService.WORDING_VERSION` identifies the text that an account accepted.
That text is the first-login screen, published in a signed release and immutable in git.
The webapp holds the same constant beside the text.
It submits its own constant.
Thus, a browser tab that predates a deployment cannot record acceptance of text it never rendered.

A fork that changes the text must change both constants in the same commit.
Such a change gives every account one more setup screen.
Make it on purpose.

## Operator obligations

Under [Art. 4(7) GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), the controller determines the purposes and means of processing.
Use of Hephaestus does not make the upstream project your controller.
[§ 5 DDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ddg/__5.html) applies to qualifying German digital services.
Links to the upstream notices do not describe a separately operated deployment.

1. Identify the organization responsible for your deployment and its processing purposes.
2. Publish a privacy notice with the information that Arts. 13 and 14 GDPR require.
   It must include data about contributors who never sign in.
3. Publish the operator identification that your jurisdiction requires.

## Three-layer resolution

At page load, the webapp fetches the relevant Markdown file in this priority order.
It uses the first file it finds:

| Priority | Path                                        | Typical source                         |
| -------: | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
|       1. | `/legal-overrides/<page>.md`                | Bind-mounted by the operator           |
|       2. | `/legal/profiles/<LEGAL_PROFILE>/<page>.md` | Baked into the image (bundled profile) |
|       3. | `/legal/_disclaimer/<page>.md`              | Shipped safety-fallback                |

`<page>` is `imprint` or `privacy`.
A banner alerts end users whenever the webapp serves the disclaimer.

## Choosing a configuration mode

### Mode A — Bundled profile (operators of `hephaestus.build`)

The bundled texts describe the deployment in the [data-protection records](./dsms).

1. If your deployment is the canonical TUM / AET deployment, set `LEGAL_PROFILE=tumaet` in your environment.
2. Verify that your enabled services and processing still match those records.

```env
LEGAL_PROFILE=tumaet
```

Other operators must **not** select `LEGAL_PROFILE=tumaet`.
The `tumaet` profile names TU München and the AET research group as the responsible parties.
This would be legally inaccurate for any other deployment.

### Mode B — Deployment-specific overrides (recommended for forks)

This is the recommended mode for self-hosters.
You write two Markdown files and bind-mount them into the webapp container.
They override the bundled profile, if present, and the safety-fallback.

1. Create a directory next to your Compose file:

   ```text
   legal-overrides/
   ├── imprint.md
   └── privacy.md
   ```

2. Mount the directory read-only into the webapp container at `/usr/share/nginx/html/legal-overrides`:

   ```yaml
   services:
     webapp:
       environment:
         LEGAL_PROFILE: ""
       volumes:
         - ./legal-overrides:/usr/share/nginx/html/legal-overrides:ro
   ```

   On Kubernetes, project the Markdown files into the same path through a `ConfigMap` volume.
   The simplest form mounts the whole ConfigMap as the directory:

   ```yaml
   volumes:
     - name: legal-overrides
       configMap:
         name: hephaestus-legal-overrides # keys: imprint.md, privacy.md
   volumeMounts:
     - name: legal-overrides
       mountPath: /usr/share/nginx/html/legal-overrides
       readOnly: true
   ```

   A whole-ConfigMap mount hides the directory's original contents.
   For an overlay of only some files, use `items` with `subPath`, or a `projected` volume.

3. On Coolify, add `/usr/share/nginx/html/legal-overrides` as a **Bind Mount**, not a Volume.
   The files remain editable from the host filesystem.
   The webapp checks file paths on each request.
   Thus, you can update the files in the mount without a redeploy.

Resolution is per page: a single-file override leaves the other page on the next available layer. Verify both pages identify the correct operator.

### Mode C — Built-in fallback (developer default, not a production configuration)

If `LEGAL_PROFILE` is empty and no overrides are mounted, the webapp serves a clear placeholder (`_disclaimer/*.md`).
It tells visitors that no legal content is configured.
It asks them to contact the operator directly.
The UI shows a red banner.

This is **not** a valid imprint or privacy notice.
You must not use it in production.
It is for local development and visible identification of misconfigured deployments.

## Authoring guidelines for a custom privacy notice

Use Art. 13 GDPR as the outline. Hephaestus processes personal data in at least the following categories, which your privacy notice must cover:

- **Identity and authentication** — Hephaestus-native auth federates to GitHub / LRZ GitLab.
  Each federated identity stores the provider, stable subject id, login, email-at-signup, and display name (ADR 0017).
- **Repository synchronization** — each configured repository supplies pull/merge requests, issues, reviews, review comments, commit metadata, and author profile information.
  The notice must state the retention triggers in the [record of processing](./dsms/record-of-processing.md).
  The mirror is erased when the last workspace stops monitoring the source repository.
  Disconnection of the workspace's integration or a workspace purge also erases it.
- **Account settings and email notifications** — user preferences and the provider-verified primary email address for account-security and deletion email.
  With a configured relay, this also includes optional notification subscriptions, survey invitation records, and unsubscribe capabilities.
  The notice must name the relay operator as a recipient.
- **AI-assisted features** — if enabled: messages sent to the configured LLM provider, persisted conversations, generated observations.
- **Server logs** — IP address and request metadata.
- **Error telemetry** — if you run a Sentry or equivalent instance, the notice must explicitly state this.
  It must link to the instance's retention policy.
- **Public activity page** — if you allow it, use the [template below](#the-public-activity-page).
- **Workspace addresses** — if you turn them on, use the [template below](#workspace-addresses).

Determine a lawful basis for each processing purpose with your data-protection adviser.
Neither self-hosting nor acceptance of terms supplies one automatically. Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR does not apply to public
authorities processing data in the performance of their tasks.

For international transfers, document the applicable Chapter V safeguard for the actual recipient,
service and destination. Verify current adequacy decisions, certification scope where relevant, and
contractual safeguards rather than inferring coverage from a provider's brand.

### The public activity page

**Allow public activity pages** is off by default.
When it is on, a workspace admin can publish the activity of the workspace's public repositories to anyone.
The page lists members and outside contributors who never signed in.
Each person can hide.
The [DPIA pre-screen](./dsms/dpia-prescreen.md#reassessment-the-public-activity-page) gives the TUM reasoning.

Before you turn on the setting:

1. Determine a lawful basis for the publication with your data-protection adviser.
2. If you are a public body, record the task for which the publication is necessary.
3. In the German private sector, check entries 7 and 8 of the [DSK list](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/ah/20181017_ah_DSK_DSFA_Muss-Liste_Version_1.1_Deutsch.pdf).
4. If the people on the page are employees, check employment law. In Germany, a works council co-determines it ([§ 87(1) no. 6 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__87.html)).
5. Add the text below to your privacy notice.
6. Tell workspace admins to keep course workspaces private, and to tell the members before they publish a page.
7. Give your privacy contact a way to hide a person who has no account.

```md
### Public activity page

If a workspace administrator publishes a public activity page, anyone can read it without sign-in.
For each person who contributed to the workspace's public repositories, it shows the display name,
login and avatar; the pull requests opened, merged and reviewed, the issues opened and the active weeks
in a selected period; a Contributions total, a position number and links to the work. The data comes
from the public repositories on [GitHub or GitLab]. The page never shows practices, observations,
feedback, Slack or Outline content, or AI review content.

Purpose: [purpose]. Legal basis: [legal basis]. The page is computed on each request and holds no
copy. Responses can be cached for up to 60 seconds.

**Right to object.** You can object at any time to your appearance on public activity pages. Turn off
**Show me on public activity pages** in User settings, or select **Hide me** when a workspace with a
public activity page asks. Without an account, contact [privacy contact]. We stop at once, and you
also leave every total on the page.
```

### Workspace addresses

With workspace addresses on, each workspace has its own host, such as `team.example.org`.
Sign-in and its cookies stay on the instance host.
Each workspace host keeps its own cookie choice and theme in the browser.
A wildcard certificate through the DNS-01 challenge needs no proxy, and the DNS provider receives no request data.
If a proxy or CDN serves the hosts, it is a recipient.
Name it in the notice.

```md
A workspace can have its own address, such as [team.example.org]. Sign-in and its cookies stay on
[example.org]. Each workspace address stores its own cookie choice and theme in your browser, so it
asks for your cookie choice once. [If a proxy or CDN serves these addresses: its provider, role and
transfer basis.]
```

## Verifying the configuration

A 200 status is not sufficient.
SPA-catchall proxies frequently return `200 text/html` for missing files.

1. After deployment, confirm that the correct layer serves each page.
2. Check both the status **and** the content type:

   ```sh
   curl -sI https://<your-host>/legal-overrides/privacy.md \
     | grep -E '^(HTTP|Content-Type)'   # expect 200 + text/markdown if overrides are mounted
   curl -sI https://<your-host>/legal/profiles/<profile>/privacy.md \
     | grep -E '^(HTTP|Content-Type)'
   curl -sI https://<your-host>/legal/_disclaimer/privacy.md \
     | grep -E '^(HTTP|Content-Type)'   # always 200 + text/markdown
   ```

3. Fetch the body of the layer that you expect to serve the page:
   Confirm that it is your content.

   ```sh
   curl -s https://<your-host>/imprint | head -c 200   # SPA shell
   curl -s https://<your-host>/legal-overrides/privacy.md | head -n 3   # should start with your heading
   ```

4. Open `/imprint` in a browser.
5. Open `/privacy` in a browser.

If the red "not configured" banner appears, the webapp serves the disclaimer.
You must still configure the legal pages.

## Changing the texts over time

Markdown files are static resources.
Overrides under `/legal-overrides/` use `Cache-Control: no-cache, must-revalidate`.
An edit on the host takes effect on the next page load.
Browsers revalidate every time.

The bundled `/legal/` layer uses `max-age=300`.
Edits to a bundled profile require a rebuild and up to five minutes for caches to clear.
No extra registry needs maintenance.

1. For a new bundled profile that replaces a previous one, update the text under `webapp/public/legal/profiles/<profile>/` in place.
2. Rebuild the image.

### Email activation and the TUM profile

The bundled TUM privacy profile describes TUM/LRZ mail services, not an arbitrary SMTP provider.
Configuration and a successful test email are technical checks, not institutional approval.
Self-hosters must publish their own applicable recipient information instead of TUM's notice.

1. Keep email disabled until the deployment record confirms the actual service, institutional arrangement, and relay retention terms.
2. If you use a different relay, update the public notice's recipient and transfer information before you enable it.
