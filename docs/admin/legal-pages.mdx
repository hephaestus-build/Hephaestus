Hephaestus serves two legal pages: `/imprint` and `/privacy`.
Until you publish your own, it serves a placeholder that says that no legal content is configured, with a red banner.
Publish both pages before the first user signs in.

:::caution[CAUTION: Configure legal notices before admitting users]
The built-in placeholder pages are not legal notices.
On a deployment that serves the placeholder, the first-login screen links to no legal notice.
:::

## Before you start

You are the controller of your deployment under [Art. 4(7) GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), not the upstream project.
Links to the upstream notices do not describe your deployment.

- Identify the organization responsible for your deployment and its purposes.
- Determine a lawful basis for each purpose with your data-protection adviser. Neither self-hosting nor acceptance of the terms supplies one. Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR does not apply to public authorities in the performance of their tasks.
- Find out which operator identification your jurisdiction requires, such as [§ 5 DDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ddg/__5.html) for qualifying German digital services.

## Write the privacy notice

Use Art. 13 GDPR as the outline, and include the information that Art. 14 GDPR requires for contributors who never sign in.
Your notice must cover at least these categories:

- **Identity and sign-in**: the provider, ID, login, sign-up email address, and display name of each linked GitHub or GitLab identity.
- **Repository synchronization**: pull and merge requests, issues, reviews, review comments, commit metadata, and author profiles of each monitored repository. State the retention triggers that [Data retention](./data-retention) lists.
- **Account settings and email**: preferences and the verified primary email address. With a mail relay, also notification subscriptions. Name the relay operator as a recipient.
- **AI features**: messages to the configured model providers, stored conversations, and practice results.
- **Server logs**: IP addresses and request metadata.
- **Log collection**: if you ship container logs to a store such as Loki ([Collect logs](./monitoring.md#collect-logs)), name its operator and retention.
- **Optional features** that you turn on: use the texts in [Notice texts for optional features](#notice-texts-for-optional-features).

For each transfer outside the EU or EEA, document the Chapter V safeguard for the actual recipient, service, and destination.

## Publish your pages

### Deployment-specific overrides

1. Write `imprint.md` and `privacy.md` in Markdown.
2. Put both files in a ConfigMap in the release namespace:

   ```bash
   kubectl --namespace hephaestus create configmap hephaestus-legal-overrides \
     --from-file=imprint.md --from-file=privacy.md
   ```

3. Name the ConfigMap in your values file, then run the install command again:

   ```yaml title="values.yaml"
   webapp:
     legalOverrides: hephaestus-legal-overrides
   ```

The chart mounts the ConfigMap read-only at `/usr/share/nginx/html/legal-overrides`.
The webapp Pod waits until the ConfigMap exists.
A webapp container outside the chart reads the same two files from that path. The files must be readable by UID 101.

The webapp reads the files on each page load.
Kubernetes updates a mounted ConfigMap within about a minute, so a later edit needs no redeploy.
Each page is resolved on its own: an override of one file leaves the other page on the placeholder.

## Check that it worked

A 200 status alone proves nothing, because a catch-all route returns `200 text/html` for a missing file.

1. Check the status and the content type of the override:

   ```sh
   curl -sI https://<your-host>/legal-overrides/privacy.md | grep -E '^(HTTP|Content-Type)'
   ```

   Expect `200` and `text/markdown`.
2. Check that the file starts with your own heading:

   ```sh
   curl -s https://<your-host>/legal-overrides/privacy.md | head -n 3
   ```

3. Open `/imprint` and `/privacy` in a browser. Check that no red "not configured" banner appears and that both name the correct operator.

Overrides are served with `Cache-Control: no-cache, must-revalidate`, so an edit shows on the next page load.

## The first-login screen

The first-login screen carries no operator-specific text.
It states the terms of use and links to `/privacy` and `/imprint`.
When the person selects **Save and continue**, Hephaestus records the acceptance of **I accept the terms of use**.
The record names the notice version shown.
It does not record that anybody opened a link.
[Your data in Hephaestus](/user/privacy#signing-in-and-choosing-research-participation) describes the screen for the person who answers it.

## The optional research question

`config.researchOrganization` names the organization that runs research on your deployment ([Values](./helm-chart#values)).
While it is empty, the default, no research question appears, and the research switch in **User settings** is hidden.
With a name, the first-login screen asks the research question as a separate decision, and either answer completes setup.
Choose a name that reads correctly in "If you say yes, the organization may use your data for research."

Before you set it, meet the duties in [Run a research program](./research-program#what-operators-must-do).

### What the question asks

The screen asks **Allow research use of your data?**, with **Yes, allow research use** and **No, keep my data out of research**.
The configured organization appears beside the choice, because consent must name the controller ([EDPB 05/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202005_consent_en.pdf) § 3.3).

The legal basis is consent under Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR: a broad consent to an area of research, not to one project ([Recital 33 GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-33/)).
The area is how developers work and learn, and how AI systems can review and support that work, including benchmarks and evaluation datasets.
The research may use the participant's work in the connected tools, and the Hephaestus results and feedback about that work.
It may also use the participant's responses and how they use Hephaestus, including conversations.
It never uses sign-in credentials, access tokens, or Slack messages that the participant stopped Hephaestus from using.

Saying no or withdrawing has no disadvantage ([Art. 7(4) GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-7-gdpr/)).

Research outside this area needs a new wording after legal review.

### When a new decision is required

A change of `config.researchOrganization` is a new question.
After a rename, earlier grants authorize nothing, and setup asks the research question again.
Earlier decisions stay in the record, and terms acceptance stays unchanged.

A wording that adds a research purpose, a recipient, a use of data, or a kind of research needs a new decision.
A narrower wording can keep a decision that still covers every retained purpose for the same organization.
A screen left open across a change cannot record a decision: Hephaestus refuses it with `409`.

### Withdrawal

A participant withdraws with **Allow research use of my data** in **User settings → Research participation**.
New research use stops at once, and you remove the participant's data from datasets that are not anonymized.
Research done before the withdrawal stays lawful, and anonymized published data cannot be removed.
Withdrawal does not erase other data. Account erasure is a separate request.

## Notice texts for optional features

### The public activity page

**Allow public activity pages** is off by default.
When it is on, a workspace admin can publish the activity of the workspace's public repositories to anyone.
The page names a person with an account only after that person chooses to show.
It counts contributors without an account but does not name them, unless the workspace admin turns on **Show contributors without an account**.
Each named person can hide.

Before you turn on the setting:

1. Determine a lawful basis for the publication with your data-protection adviser.
2. If you are a public body, record the task for which the publication is necessary.
3. In the German private sector, check entries 7 and 8 of the [DSK list](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/ah/20181017_ah_DSK_DSFA_Muss-Liste_Version_1.1_Deutsch.pdf).
4. If the people on the page are employees, check employment law. In Germany, a works council co-determines it ([§ 87(1) no. 6 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__87.html)).
5. Add the text below to your privacy notice.
6. Tell workspace admins to keep course workspaces private, and to tell the members before they publish a page.
7. Give your privacy contact a way to hide a person who has no account.

```md
### Public activity page

If a workspace administrator publishes a public activity page, anyone can read it without sign-in.
A person with an account is on the page only after they choose to show. Contributors without an
account are counted but not named, unless the workspace administrator chooses to name them.
For each person on the page, it shows the display name, login and avatar; the pull requests opened,
merged and reviewed, the issues opened and the active weeks in a selected period; a Contributions
total, a position number and links to the work. The data comes from the public repositories on
[GitHub or GitLab]. The page never shows practices, practice results, feedback, Slack or Outline
content, or AI review content.

Purpose: [purpose]. Legal basis: [legal basis]. The page is computed on each request and holds no
copy.

**Right to object.** You can object at any time to your appearance on public activity pages. Turn off
**Show me on public activity pages** in User settings, or select **Hide me** when a workspace with a
public activity page asks. Without an account, contact [privacy contact]. We stop at once, and you
also leave every total on the page.
```

### Page experience

**`config.pageExperienceMetrics`** is off by default.
When it is on, the browser of each signed-in reader measures how fast each page loaded and responded.
It sends new measurements to your server when the reader leaves the page, and it sends each measurement once.
The server adds them to counters that name no person, workspace or page address ([Page experience metrics](./metrics.md#page-experience-metrics)).
It keeps no record of the report.
The report is a request, so it carries the same request metadata as every other request.

In the EU, turning it on can require consent or a documented legal basis.
The browser reads these timings from the reader's device.
[Art. 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02002L0058-20091219) and its national laws, such as [§ 25 TDDDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ttdsg/__25.html) in Germany, cover such access.
Some regulators exempt narrow performance measurement under conditions, and others do not.
Whether an exemption applies depends on your national law, its regulator and how you run the instance.

For the processing of the report, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in the performance of the service) is one possible basis.
Hephaestus shows no consent prompt.

You decide with your data-protection adviser, and you record the decision in your privacy notice.
Before you turn it on:

1. Determine with your data-protection adviser whether your national ePrivacy law permits the access without consent.
2. If it requires consent, keep the setting off.
3. Determine and record a lawful basis for the processing of the report.
4. Add the text below to your privacy notice.
5. Set `config.pageExperienceMetrics: true` in the chart values.

```md
### Page experience

Your browser measures how fast each page loads and responds, and how much the layout moves. When you
leave the page, it sends the new timings to [example.org]. They are counted without your name,
workspace or page address, and no copy of the report is kept. Like every request, the report reaches
the server with your IP address and browser information, which the server logs keep for
[retention]. Purpose: [purpose]. Legal basis: [legal basis].
```

### Browser fault reports

**`config.browserFaultReports`** is off by default.
When it is on, the browser of each reader reports the failures that it meets on a page, also before sign-in.
A report names the kind of failure, the page type, the class of the error, the release and positions in the web app's scripts.
For a failed request, it also names the request's reference and its status code.
It names no person, workspace, page address or error message ([Browser faults](./metrics.md#browser-faults)).
The browser stores nothing on the device for it.

The server writes each failure to its log and counts it. It keeps no other record.
The report is a request, so it carries the same request metadata as every other request.

The browser reads these failures on the reader's device, so the same ePrivacy rules apply as for [Page experience](#page-experience).
Error reports serve the operation and security of the service that the reader uses, not the measurement of its audience.
Many operators rely on the exemption for what is strictly necessary to provide that service, such as § 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG in Germany.
Regulators do not all read that exemption the same way, so decide it for your jurisdiction.

Before you turn it on:

1. Determine with your data-protection adviser whether your national ePrivacy law permits the access without consent.
2. If it requires consent, keep the setting off.
3. Record your lawful basis for the processing of the report. A common choice is a legitimate interest in a service that works and is secure (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR), with the balancing test on file.
4. Add the text below to your privacy notice. Replace each bracketed part.
5. Set `config.browserFaultReports: true` in the chart values.

```md
### Error reports

When a page of [example.org] fails in your browser, your browser sends us a short report: the kind
of failure, the type of page, the version of the app and where in our scripts the failure happened.
If a request to our server failed, the report also contains the reference of that request and its
status code. The report contains no name, workspace, page address or error text, and nothing is
stored on your device for it.

Like every request, the report reaches our server with your IP address and browser information.
Our server logs keep them for [retention, for example 14 days]. We use the reports only to find
and fix failures of the service, and we do not combine them with other data about you.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in a service that works and is secure (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
[Access to your device: name your ePrivacy basis, for example § 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG.]
You can object to this processing at any time: [contact].
```

### Product feedback on GitHub

Product feedback stays on your instance by default.
When you name a repository under **Product feedback → Destinations** in the instance admin console, product feedback leaves your instance:

- **To your model provider for a draft.** Your instance-level model drafts an issue from each report.
  Hephaestus removes the email addresses, secrets, and known names that it finds before the text reaches the provider.
  Each report also carries the page route, which holds no name or ID, and the release. Name both in your privacy notice.
- **To GitHub, in your private repository.** Reports that stay private become issues there.
  GitHub stores them under your organization’s agreement with GitHub.
- **To GitHub, in your public repository, only with the person’s choice.** Anyone can read these issues.
  Copies outside GitHub can remain after removal.

Redaction lowers the risk, but it cannot remove it.
A report can still contain personal data that Hephaestus did not find.

Before you name a private repository:

1. Determine a lawful basis for drafts and private issues with your data-protection adviser.
   Neither self-hosting nor a person’s report supplies one automatically.
   Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR does not apply to public authorities in the performance of their tasks.
2. Check GitHub’s role under your organization’s agreement.
   GitHub’s data protection agreement makes GitHub a processor for some processing and an independent controller for other processing.
   Name GitHub as a recipient in the notice.
3. Check your model provider’s agreement under Art. 28 GDPR.
   Check the location of the processing.
   Check whether the provider uses inputs for training.
4. For each transfer outside the EU or EEA, document the Chapter V safeguard for the actual recipient and service.
5. Add the [drafting and private filing notice](#drafting-and-private-filing-notice) to your privacy notice.
6. When your notice names a new recipient or purpose, turn on **Ask everyone to choose again** under **Product feedback → Destinations**.
   It raises the notice version and clears every person's remembered choice, so each next report starts with a clear box.

Before you name a public repository, also:

1. Decide whether consent is the right basis for publication.
   If your users report to you as students or employees, assess whether their choice is free.
   The EDPB guidelines on consent (05/2020, § 3.1.1) presume an imbalance for public authorities and employers.
   Consent is valid only when a private report costs the person nothing.
   In Hephaestus, a private report reaches the same maintainers.
2. Add the [public issues notice](#public-issues-notice) to your privacy notice.
3. Check the composer in a staging instance.

The box is **Post as a public GitHub issue**, and it is clear until the person selects it.
A report sent with the box clear stays private.
The dialog names the model provider and explains redaction limits and public copies.
The [user guide](/user/product-feedback#choose-public-or-private) describes the choice and its withdrawal.

Hephaestus records each selection and each withdrawal as an event with the notice version and time (Art. 7(1) GDPR).
A report sent with the box clear records no event.
It does not decide the legal basis for you.
Replace the placeholders in both templates with your deployment’s actual recipients, agreements, legal bases, and retention.

#### Drafting and private filing notice

```md
### Product feedback

When you send product feedback about Hephaestus, we store it on [example.org] and use it to improve
Hephaestus. An AI model from [model provider] turns your report into a short issue. Before your
text goes to [model provider], Hephaestus removes the email addresses, secrets and names it finds.
It can miss some, so leave out other people's personal details. Each report also carries the page
route, which holds no name or ID, and the release.

If you keep a report private, it becomes an issue in a private GitHub repository that only the
[maintainers / team name] can read. The issue doesn't carry your name or email address. It
carries a report number, so that our administrators can contact you if they need to.

Recipients: [model provider] ([role, location, transfer basis]) and GitHub, Inc.
([role under our agreement, transfer basis]). Purpose: handling and fixing product feedback.
Legal basis: [legal basis]. We keep your report in Hephaestus until you delete your account
[or: retention]. Issues stay in the repository until the maintainers delete them. When you delete
your account, Hephaestus removes your text from those issues.
```

#### Public issues notice

```md
### Public issues from product feedback

A report stays private unless you select **Post as a public GitHub issue** when you send it. The
box is clear on your first report. If you select it, Hephaestus remembers that and starts your next
report with it selected. Clearing it applies to that report only. You can also select **Allow
public** on a private report in **Your reports**, which sets the same default. You can change the
default at any time in **User settings → Product feedback**.

Public issues go to [owner/repository]. A report becomes public only when Hephaestus finds no
security problem, no secrets, no email addresses and no names of people in it. Anything else stays
private.

A public issue doesn't carry your name, unless you choose to have your GitHub account mentioned.
Anyone can read a public issue. Before it's filed, you have 5 minutes to keep it private. After
that, you can make it private from **Your reports**: we then move it to our private repository.
Copies made outside GitHub while it was public can remain, and we can't remove them.

Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). You can withdraw it at any time in your settings.
Withdrawing doesn't affect issues that are already public. You can make each of them private.
```

### Workspace addresses

With workspace addresses on, each workspace has its own host, such as `team.example.org`.
Sign-in and its cookies stay on the instance host.
The web app asks for no cookie choice, because it sets no optional cookie.
The theme is one cookie, `hephaestus-theme`, on the workspace base domain (`Domain=`, `SameSite=Lax`, and `Secure` on HTTPS).
Thus, all workspace hosts share the light or dark mode.
It is a strictly necessary preference cookie that the reader sets, and it expires after one year.

A wildcard certificate through the DNS-01 challenge needs no proxy, and the DNS provider receives no request data.
If a proxy or CDN serves the hosts, it is a recipient.
Name it in the notice.

```md
A workspace can have its own address, such as [team.example.org]. Sign-in and its cookies stay on
[example.org]. All these addresses share one strictly necessary cookie, hephaestus-theme, that
remembers your light or dark mode for one year. [If a proxy or CDN serves these addresses: its
provider, role and transfer basis.]
```
