Hephaestus ships with two legal pages, `/imprint` and `/privacy`, that are rendered from Markdown files served directly by nginx. This page explains the three-layer resolution used by the webapp, and what each self-hosted operator is responsible for.

:::danger Configure legal notices before admitting users
The built-in “not configured” pages are placeholders, not legal notices. Use `LEGAL_PROFILE=tumaet`
only for the TUM/AET deployment it describes; other operators must supply their own notices.
:::

## First-login consent notice

The first-login screen carries no operator-specific text. It states the terms of use and points to
`/privacy` and `/imprint` for who runs the deployment, what it stores and for how long, so the same
wording is accurate on every instance and these overrides are what make it complete.

The sign-in and first-login screens carry their own `/privacy` and `/imprint` links, because they
render without the site footer. **Configure both pages before the first user signs in**: on a
deployment that still serves the placeholder, the first-login screen points at nothing.

Submitting the screen records, against the notice version shown, that the account accepted the terms
and that this wording — which links the privacy notice — was put in front of it. Both come from the one
checkbox: the ledger evidences what the account was shown and agreed to, not that anybody opened the
link. A research answer is recorded as a third, separate decision where the question is asked at all
(below), and either answer completes setup. Nothing is recorded until the account holder submits.
[Your data in Hephaestus](/user/privacy#signing-in-and-choosing-research-participation) describes the
same screen for the person answering it.

## The optional research question

`HEPHAESTUS_RESEARCH_ORGANIZATION` names the organisation that runs a study on this deployment, and its
presence is what decides whether there is one. Consent has to be informed, and the identity of the
controller is part of that ([EDPB 05/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202005_consent_en.pdf)
§ 3.3), so the name you set is shown beside the choice on the first-login screen and in account
settings rather than left to a link.

```env
HEPHAESTUS_RESEARCH_ORGANIZATION="Technical University of Munich (AET)"
```

Leave it unset — the default — and no question is asked: first-login setup is the terms alone, the
account-settings switch is hidden, and `PUT /user/consent/research` answers 404. Decisions already
recorded stay in the ledger untouched.

Hephaestus records the answer; it does not enforce it. No server code reads the decision to exclude a
refusing or withdrawn account from processing, so an operator running a study must apply the current
decision in their own exports and analyses. Note also that the Slack app home reads the separate
`user_preferences.participate_in_research` flag, which this screen does not write. The recorded answer
is evidence of what was asked, not a technical control.

`ConsentService.CURRENT_NOTICE_VERSION` identifies the wording an account accepted; the wording itself
is the first-login screen, published in a signed release and immutable in git. The webapp holds the
same constant beside those words and submits its own, so a browser tab that predates a deployment
cannot record acceptance of wording it never rendered. A fork that changes the words changes both
constants in the same commit.

## Operator obligations

Identify the organisation responsible for your deployment and its processing purposes. Under
[Art. 4(7) GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), the controller is the party that determines
the purposes and means of processing; using Hephaestus does not make the upstream project your controller.

Publish a privacy notice covering the information required by Arts. 13 and 14 GDPR, including data about
contributors who never sign in. Publish the operator identification required in your jurisdiction;
[§ 5 DDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ddg/__5.html) applies to qualifying German digital services.
Linking to the upstream notices does not describe a separately operated deployment.

## Three-layer resolution

At page load, the webapp fetches the relevant Markdown file in this priority order — first hit wins:

| Priority | Path                                        | Typical source                         |
| -------: | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
|       1. | `/legal-overrides/<page>.md`                | Bind-mounted by the operator           |
|       2. | `/legal/profiles/<LEGAL_PROFILE>/<page>.md` | Baked into the image (bundled profile) |
|       3. | `/legal/_disclaimer/<page>.md`              | Shipped safety-fallback                |

`<page>` is `imprint` or `privacy`. A banner alerts end users whenever the disclaimer is being served.

## Choosing a configuration mode

### Mode A — Bundled profile (operators of `hephaestus.build`)

If your deployment is the canonical TUM / AET deployment, set `LEGAL_PROFILE=tumaet` in your environment. The bundled texts describe the deployment documented in the [data-protection records](./dsms). Verify that your enabled services and processing still match those records.

```env
LEGAL_PROFILE=tumaet
```

Other operators must **not** select `LEGAL_PROFILE=tumaet` — the `tumaet` profile names TU München and the AET research group as the responsible parties and would be legally inaccurate for any other deployment.

### Mode B — Deployment-specific overrides (recommended for forks)

This is the recommended mode for self-hosters. You author two Markdown files and bind-mount them into the webapp container. They shadow the bundled profile (if any) and the safety-fallback.

1. Create a directory next to your Compose file:

   ```text
   legal-overrides/
   ├── imprint.md
   └── privacy.md
   ```

2. Mount the directory read-only into the webapp container at `/usr/share/nginx/html/legal-overrides`:

   ```yaml
   services:
     webapp:
       environment:
         LEGAL_PROFILE: ""
       volumes:
         - ./legal-overrides:/usr/share/nginx/html/legal-overrides:ro
   ```

   On Kubernetes, project the Markdown files into the same path via a `ConfigMap` volume. The simplest form mounts the whole ConfigMap as the directory:

   ```yaml
   volumes:
     - name: legal-overrides
       configMap:
         name: hephaestus-legal-overrides # keys: imprint.md, privacy.md
   volumeMounts:
     - name: legal-overrides
       mountPath: /usr/share/nginx/html/legal-overrides
       readOnly: true
   ```

   To overlay only some of the files onto an existing directory, use `items` with `subPath`, or a
   `projected` volume — a whole-ConfigMap mount hides the directory's original contents.

3. On Coolify, add `/usr/share/nginx/html/legal-overrides` as a **Bind Mount** (not a Volume) so the files remain editable from the host filesystem. The file paths are checked on each request, so you can update them by editing the files in the mount — no redeploy needed.

Resolution is per page: a single-file override leaves the other page on the next available layer. Verify both pages identify the correct operator.

### Mode C — Built-in fallback (developer default, not a production configuration)

If `LEGAL_PROFILE` is empty and no overrides are mounted, the webapp serves a clearly-worded placeholder (`_disclaimer/*.md`) that tells visitors no legal content has been configured and asks them to contact the operator directly. A red banner is shown in the UI. This is **not** a valid imprint or privacy statement and must not be used in production — it is intended for local development and for catching misconfigured deployments loudly rather than silently.

## Authoring guidelines for a custom privacy statement

Use Art. 13 GDPR as the outline. Hephaestus processes personal data in at least the following categories, which your privacy statement must cover:

- **Identity and authentication** — Hephaestus-native auth federating to GitHub / LRZ GitLab; per federated identity we store the provider, stable subject id, login, email-at-signup, and display name (ADR 0017).
- **Repository synchronisation** — for every configured repository: pull/merge requests, issues, reviews, review comments, commit metadata, profile information of the authors. State the retention triggers: this mirrored copy is erased when the last workspace stops monitoring the source repository, when the workspace's integration is disconnected, or when the workspace is purged — see the erasure triggers in the [record of processing](./dsms/record-of-processing.md).
- **Account settings and notification email** — user preferences, notification subscriptions, optional self-provided notification email.
- **AI-assisted features** — if enabled: messages sent to the configured LLM provider, persisted conversations, generated observations.
- **Server logs** — IP address and request metadata.
- **Error telemetry** — if you run a Sentry (or equivalent) instance, state so explicitly and link its retention policy.

Determine a lawful basis for each processing purpose with your data-protection adviser; neither
self-hosting nor accepting terms supplies one automatically. Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR does not apply to public
authorities processing data in the performance of their tasks.

For international transfers, document the applicable Chapter V safeguard for the actual recipient,
service and destination. Verify current adequacy decisions, certification scope where relevant, and
contractual safeguards rather than inferring coverage from a provider's brand.

## Verifying the configuration

After deploying, confirm the correct layer is serving each page. A 200 status is not sufficient — SPA-catchall proxies frequently return `200 text/html` for missing files. Check both the status **and** the content type:

```sh
curl -sI https://<your-host>/legal-overrides/privacy.md \
  | grep -E '^(HTTP|Content-Type)'   # expect 200 + text/markdown if overrides are mounted
curl -sI https://<your-host>/legal/profiles/<profile>/privacy.md \
  | grep -E '^(HTTP|Content-Type)'
curl -sI https://<your-host>/legal/_disclaimer/privacy.md \
  | grep -E '^(HTTP|Content-Type)'   # always 200 + text/markdown
```

Then fetch the body of the layer you expect to be served and confirm it is your content:

```sh
curl -s https://<your-host>/imprint | head -c 200   # SPA shell
curl -s https://<your-host>/legal-overrides/privacy.md | head -n 3   # should start with your heading
```

Finally, open `/imprint` and `/privacy` in a browser. If the red "not configured" banner appears, the disclaimer is being served and you still owe a configuration.

## Changing the texts over time

Markdown files are static resources. Overrides under `/legal-overrides/` are served with `Cache-Control: no-cache, must-revalidate`, so an edit on the host takes effect on the next page load (browsers revalidate every time). The bundled `/legal/` layer is served with `max-age=300`, so edits to a bundled profile require a rebuild and up to five minutes for caches to clear. When you ship a new bundled profile that replaces a previous one, update the text in place under `webapp/public/legal/profiles/<profile>/` and rebuild the image; there is no extra registry to maintain.
